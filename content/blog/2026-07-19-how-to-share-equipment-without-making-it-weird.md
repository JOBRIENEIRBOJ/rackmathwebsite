# How to Share Equipment Without Making It Weird

Standing there, staring at the squat rack you need, while someone else uses it, can feel like a social test you did not study for.

Do you wait? Ask to “work in”? Leave and do something else?

You are not the only one who feels this way. A lot of beginners say the gym makes them anxious, not because of the weights, but because of the people and unwritten rules.

## The simple truth

Most people in the gym are happy to share equipment.

They just expect you to be:

- Clear
- Polite
- Quick

If you can do those three things, you will be fine.

## Why this matters

When you avoid equipment because someone is on it, your workout turns into random machines and whatever is free.

Over time, that can make it harder to build a simple, consistent strength routine.

Major health organizations recommend adults do muscle‑strengthening activity at least two days per week, because it helps support muscle, bone, and daily function over time.[^1][^2][^3]

You cannot get that benefit if you keep skipping the lifts you planned because you are nervous to ask for a rack or bench.

Learning how to share equipment removes one more barrier between you and a solid workout.

## What beginners usually get wrong

A few common patterns show up:

- **Waiting forever.** You hover nearby, pretend to stretch, and hope they magically notice you. They probably won’t.
- **Assuming “no.”** You tell yourself they will be annoyed if you ask to work in, so you never ask.
- **Jumping in without asking.** You grab plates or sit on a machine during someone’s rest. That *will* annoy people.
- **Apologizing too much.** You treat a normal question like you are asking for a huge favor.

You do not need a perfect script or perfect timing.

You just need a simple way to ask, and a plan for what to do if they say yes or no.

## What to do instead

### 1. Know the basic rule

If someone is:

- Sitting on the machine between sets  
- Standing right at the bar  
- Clearly mid‑set or about to start  

That means they are still using it.

If they are:

- Walking away with their bag  
- Wiping it down  
- Nowhere near the station  

You can walk up, look around briefly, and then use it. If you are unsure, ask:

> “Hey, are you still using this?”

### 2. Use one simple question

Here are three easy lines you can memorize:

- “Hey, how many sets do you have left?”
- “Mind if I work in between your sets?”
- “When you’re done, can I grab this next?”

Pick one that feels easiest to say. That is enough.

Say it with a normal voice, quick eye contact, and a small nod or smile if that feels natural. Then wait.

Most common answers:

- “Yeah, go ahead and work in.”
- “I’ve got two sets left.”
- “I’m almost done, this is my last one.”

All of those are wins. Now you know what to do next.

### 3. If they say “you can work in”

“Working in” means you take turns using the same equipment between sets.

Here is how to do that without making it weird:

1. **Confirm quickly.**  
   “Cool, thanks, I’ll work in between your sets.”

2. **Match their rhythm.**  
   - When they lift, you stay out of the way.  
   - When they finish a set and step back, you step in and lift.  
   - When you finish, you move away so they can go again.

3. **Change the weight fast.**  
   - Say what you are doing: “I’m just going to drop it down to 95 and I’ll put it back for you.”  
   - Remove or add plates smoothly.  
   - Put it back to *their* weight when you are done with your set.

4. **Keep your stuff tidy.**  
   Don’t spread your bag, water, and notebook all over the area. Keep it in one spot.

5. **Don’t take long breaks.**  
   Do your set, rest off to the side, and be ready when it is your turn again.

If you are unsure about their weight, just ask:

> “What weight are you using? I’ll put it back to that after my set.”

That sentence alone makes you seem experienced, even if you are new.

### 4. If they say “I have a few sets left”

You have two good options.

**Option A: Wait nearby on purpose**

- Say: “No worries, I’ll wait.”  
- Stay close enough that they can see you are next, but not right in their space.  
- Use the time to fill your water, stretch, or plan your next set.

**Option B: Do something else and come back**

- Say: “Cool, I’ll come back after a couple other sets.”  
- Go do a different exercise (like dumbbells or a machine) that trains a similar muscle group.
- Check back in 5–10 minutes.

This keeps your workout moving without giving up on the lift you wanted.

### 5. If they say “no” or act annoyed

Sometimes someone really does not want to share.

They might say:

- “I’m on a tight timer, sorry.”
- “I’d rather not, I’m doing something specific.”

You can just reply:

> “All good, thanks.”

Then walk away and do something else.

You do not need to explain yourself or feel bad. One person’s mood does not say anything about whether you “belong” in the gym.

### 6. How to share different types of equipment

**Squat racks and bench presses**

- These are slower to share because you change a lot of weight.  
- Working in is easiest if your weights are somewhat close.  
- If their weight is very different and you are nervous about changing plates, you can say:  
  > “I’ll grab another station, but could I take this after you’re done?”

**Cable machines**

- Often have two sides. You can use one side while they use the other.  
- If they are using both, ask:  
  > “Can I use this side for rows while you do your sets?”

**Dumbbells**

- Easy: grab another pair in a nearby weight if your exact weight is taken.  
- If you really need that weight, you can ask:  
  > “Are you using these 20s? If not, can I grab them?”

**Benches**

- If someone is sitting and scrolling for a long time, ask:  
  > “Hey, are you still using this bench?”  
- If yes, ask:  
  > “Cool, about how many sets do you have left?”

### 7. Sharing when you are new and lifting lighter

You might worry:

> “They will think I’m weak if I change the weight down.”

Most people do not care how much you lift. They care:

- That you are safe
- That you put plates back
- That you are not wasting their time

If you are lifting much lighter, you can:

- Use only the inside part of the rack if it has safety arms, while they use the outside (in some gyms).  
- Ask to take it when they are done, instead of working in.

But it is also completely fine to be the person taking off plates and then putting them back on.

Everyone has been the “lighter weight” person at some point.

### 8. Basic gym sharing manners

If you follow these, you will avoid 95% of weirdness:

- **Ask before touching someone else’s bar, plates, or dumbbells.**
- **Put equipment back where you found it.**
- **Wipe down benches and pads if you sweat on them.**
- **Keep your phone use short while on equipment.**
- **Don’t camp on popular stations during rush hour.**

That is it. Not complicated. Just basic respect.

You are not weird for feeling nervous about sharing equipment.

Use a simple line, be clear and polite, move with purpose, and put the weight back.

That is how you stop circling the rack and start training like you belong there—because you do.
