How it works

Calculate plates without doing gym-floor arithmetic.

RackMath subtracts the bar weight from your target weight, divides the remaining load across both sleeves, and builds the closest plate setup from the plates you selected.

If the selected plate sizes cannot make the exact target, this free calculator shows the closest achievable total at or below it. See how to calculate plates on a barbell for the full formula and worked examples.

Equipment scope

Use the weights that are actually on your bar.

The free calculator includes Olympic, standard, training, and common specialty bars, plus a custom bar-weight option and standard plates down to 2.5 lb or 1.25 kg. It assumes each selected plate size is available as needed; it does not count a finite inventory.

Collars and finite plate counts are not added in this free tool. Check what your bar weighs, then use the custom equipment setup when collars, unlisted fractional plates, or available plate counts differ. Follow the barbell loading and safety guide while changing plates.

How do I calculate plates for a barbell?

Subtract the bar weight from your target weight, divide the remaining weight by two, then load that amount on each side using your available plates.

Does this plate calculator work for kilograms?

Yes. Switch the unit control to kg to use common 20 kg and 15 kg bars with kilogram plates.

What if my target weight cannot be loaded exactly?

The calculator shows the closest achievable weight at or below your target based on the plate sizes you selected, so you can adjust the set without doing extra math.

Does the free calculator include collars or specialty bars?

The free web calculator uses only the listed bars and plate sizes and does not add collar weight. Use RackMath's custom equipment setup for collars, specialty bars, other fractional plates, or a finite plate inventory.
